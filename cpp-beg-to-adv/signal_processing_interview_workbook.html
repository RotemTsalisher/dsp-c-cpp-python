Signal Processing & Embedded Systems Interview Workbook

This workbook progresses from fundamentals to expert-level interview challenges. Exercises combine DSP, fixed-point design, ML, embedded systems, control, and system-level reasoning.

🟢 Easy Level

Exercise 1 – Sampling & Aliasing
Explain what aliasing is. A 30 kHz sine wave is sampled at 48 kHz. What frequency appears in the discrete signal?
Aliasing occurs when sampling below Nyquist. Alias = |f - kFs| → |30 - 48| = 18 kHz.
Exercise 2 – FIR vs IIR
List two advantages and two disadvantages of FIR filters compared to IIR filters.
FIR: always stable, linear phase possible. Disadvantages: higher order, more computation.
Exercise 3 – Fixed Point Basics
What is quantization noise? How does word length affect it?
Quantization noise = rounding error. Longer word → smaller noise.
Exercise 4 – FFT Interpretation
You compute an FFT and see spectral leakage. Why does this happen?
Non-integer cycles in window → discontinuity → leakage.
Exercise 5 – MATLAB vs Python
Name typical use cases where Python is preferred over MATLAB and vice versa.
Python: production/ML/integration. MATLAB: rapid DSP prototyping & analysis.
Exercise 6 – Clock Recovery Concept
What problem does clock recovery solve in communication systems?
Aligns sampling instants with symbol timing.

🟡 Intermediate Level

Exercise 7 – Equalization Intuition
Why is equalization needed in communication channels?
Compensates channel distortion / ISI.
Exercise 8 – Phase Noise
How does phase noise affect demodulation?
Rotates constellation → detection errors.
Exercise 9 – Fixed‑Point Scaling
Describe how you would prevent overflow in fixed‑point DSP.
Proper scaling, saturation, headroom analysis.
Exercise 10 – Multithreading
When would multithreading improve DSP performance? When might it hurt?
Helps parallel workloads; hurts with overhead/sync costs.
Exercise 11 – Feature Extraction
Name common speech features used in ML pipelines.
MFCC, spectrogram, mel features, pitch.
Exercise 12 – SIMD / Vectorization
Why does SIMD accelerate DSP algorithms?
Parallel arithmetic on multiple samples per instruction.

🔵 Advanced Level

Exercise 13 – Timing Error Detector
Explain the role of a Timing Error Detector (TED).
Estimates sampling phase error for clock recovery loop.
Exercise 14 – System Constraints
List practical differences when moving MATLAB algorithms to RTL.
Latency, fixed-point limits, memory, pipelining.
Exercise 15 – ML vs Classical DSP
When would classical DSP outperform deep learning?
Low-data, interpretable, deterministic systems.
Exercise 16 – Control Stability
What defines stability in a feedback control loop?
Bounded output for bounded input (poles inside unit circle / left half-plane).
Exercise 17 – Real‑Time Constraints
Difference between real‑time and fast execution?
Real‑time = deterministic deadlines, not just speed.
Exercise 18 – Lab Debugging
Signal looks distorted on scope but OK in simulation. Possible causes?
Clocking, noise, quantization, analog effects, measurement setup.

🔴 Expert Level

Exercise 19 – Carrier Recovery Loop
Explain interaction between phase recovery and equalization loops.
Coupled loops; errors in one affect convergence of the other.
Exercise 20 – Numerical Precision
Describe fixed‑point noise propagation in cascaded DSP blocks.
Noise accumulates; scaling & word-length planning required.
Exercise 21 – Embedded System Design
Design trade‑offs: latency vs throughput vs power.
Lower latency → less buffering; higher throughput → parallelism; power impacted by compute/memory.
Exercise 22 – ML Deployment
Key challenges deploying DL models on edge devices?
Quantization, memory, compute, latency, robustness.
Exercise 23 – System‑Level Thinking
Why can optimizing a block degrade overall system performance?
Local optimum ≠ global optimum; interactions & bottlenecks.
Exercise 24 – Open‑Ended Design Question
Design a robust modem receiver for a noisy optical channel. Outline major blocks and risks.
Blocks: synchronization, equalization, phase recovery, FEC. Risks: noise, nonlinearity, loop coupling, quantization.
