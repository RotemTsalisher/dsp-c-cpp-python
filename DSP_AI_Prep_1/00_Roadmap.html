Exercise 0.1

Explain the role in one sentence without using the words DSP, AI, or optimization.

It is a role centered on implementing and optimizing signal-processing/ML workloads on compute platforms under accuracy and real-time constraints.

Exercise 0.2

What does “real-time” mean operationally? Give a measurable definition.

A real-time system must complete required work before its deadline; average runtime alone is insufficient.

Exercise 0.3

Why can a mathematically equivalent implementation have radically different runtime?

Hardware execution depends on memory access, cache behavior, instruction selection, vectorization, branches, pipeline dependencies, etc.

Exercise 0.4

What is the difference between latency and throughput?

Latency is time for one operation/result; throughput is work/results per unit time.

Exercise 0.5

Why is profiling part of engineering rather than an optional final step?

Without measurement you can optimize the wrong thing and may make code slower or less maintainable.

Exercise 0.6

Give one example each of compute-bound, memory-bound, and synchronization-bound code.

Compute-bound: many arithmetic operations per loaded byte. Memory-bound: waiting on data movement. Synchronization-bound: waiting for locks/barriers/events.

Exercise 0.7

Explain why fixed-point arithmetic matters in embedded DSP.

Fixed point can reduce hardware cost/power and may be required where floating point is expensive or unavailable, but introduces scaling/overflow/precision issues.

Exercise 0.8

Give a 45-second English answer to: “Tell me how you approach a performance problem.”

Strong answer: define the workload and metric, establish a correct baseline, profile, identify the bottleneck, change one thing, re-measure correctness/performance, and document the trade-off.
