Exercise 15.1

For BPSK over AWGN, what happens qualitatively to BER as Eb/N0 increases?

BER decreases rapidly as Eb/N0 increases. For coherent BPSK in ideal AWGN, BER has the familiar Q-function relationship. The key interview point is that more energy per bit relative to noise improves decision reliability.

Exercise 15.2

Why does complex baseband simplify many communication algorithms?

Representing passband signals with complex I/Q components lets many modulation, mixing, filtering, and synchronization operations be expressed at a lower effective sampling rate around baseband, separating amplitude/phase/frequency behavior cleanly.

Exercise 15.3

A receiver has a constant phase offset of 30°. What happens to a QPSK constellation?

The entire constellation rotates by 30° relative to the expected decision axes. Without compensation, decision regions can be crossed and symbol errors increase.

Exercise 15.4

A receiver has a small carrier-frequency offset. Why can the constellation appear to rotate continuously over time?

A frequency offset creates a phase error that accumulates with time. Therefore the relative phase changes progressively, appearing as a rotating constellation in complex baseband.

Exercise 15.5

Why is timing synchronization a DSP problem and not merely a protocol problem?

The receiver must choose the correct sampling instants from a continuous/discrete waveform. Timing error changes the sampled pulse shape and can increase inter-symbol interference and detection errors. Algorithms for timing recovery are signal-processing algorithms.

Exercise 15.6

Design a debugging sequence for a communication receiver whose BER is much worse than simulation.
  1. Verify bit mapping and test vectors.
  2. Check sample rates and scaling.
  3. Check I/Q ordering/sign conventions.
  4. Check synchronization.
  5. Compare intermediate waveforms against simulation.
  6. Check fixed-point quantization/overflow.
  7. Check filter coefficients and delays.
  8. Check noise/SNR calibration.
  9. Measure BER at intermediate stages to localize the first divergence.
